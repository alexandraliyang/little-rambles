# Phase 1 Interview Guide — Caregiver Conversations (n=10)

**Purpose:** determine whether the pain is real beyond the founder, which pain it actually is, and what people already pay for. This guide hunts **disconfirming evidence**. The worst possible outcome of an interview is a compliment; the best is a surprise.

**Recruiting:** 10 caregivers of children ~6–30 months. Mix: first-time and second-time parents; at least 3 you don't know personally; at least 2 who seem perfectly content with their routine (contented people are the disconfirmation source). 30–40 minutes, coffee on you.

**The three iron rules:**
1. **Do not pitch until Section 6.** The moment they know your idea, every answer becomes polite.
2. **Past behavior only.** "Would you use…" answers are worthless; "tell me about last Saturday" answers are gold.
3. **Silence is a tool.** After they answer, wait three seconds. The second answer is truer than the first.

---

## 1. Warm-up (3 min)
Tell me about your kid — age, personality, what are they into right now?

## 2. The core story (10 min) — the heart of the interview
"Walk me through last Saturday. From waking up — how did you end up doing what you did?"
Probe: Who decided? When was it decided — night before, that morning, in the moment? What else was considered? What almost happened instead?
Then: "And the Saturday before that?" (Two stories reveal patterns one can't.)
**Listen for (do not ask directly):** decision fatigue · sameness · guilt · one parent carrying the planning load · nap logistics dominating everything · money friction.

## 3. Discovery behavior (7 min)
"Last time you took them somewhere *new* — how did you find it? How long between hearing about it and going?"
"Where do ideas come from generally?" (Facebook groups, Instagram, friends, Google, ChatGPT — note which, unprompted.)
"Ever look something up and decide they were too young for it? What did you do?"
"Ever driven somewhere and it was closed, or a bust? What happened?"

## 4. Money (5 min) — the willingness-to-pay evidence
"What do you currently pay for related to activities or development — classes, memberships, apps, subscriptions?" Get actual numbers.
"What have you paid for and quit? Why?"
"What's the best money you've spent on your kid so far?"

## 5. Memory-keeping (4 min) — tests the adjacent pain hypothesis
"Do you keep any record of this stuff — baby book, journal, app, photos?"
"How do you feel about how that's going?" (Listen for the ache: 4,000 unsorted photos, the abandoned baby book.)

## 6. ONLY NOW — the concept (5 min)
One neutral sentence: "I'm exploring an app that suggests age-right outings nearby that are open right now, learns what your kid loves from one-tap logging, and quietly builds a journal of their first years."
Watch the face before the words. Then:
- "What part of that would you actually use?" (not "do you like it")
- **"What would make this useless for you?"** (the single most valuable question)
- "What do you do today instead, and what does it cost you?"
- If warm: "Beta will be free, launch will be ~$6–8/month — is that something you'd realistically keep paying for?" Note hedging verbatim.

## 7. Close
"Who's the most outings-obsessed parent you know?" (referral) · "Can I show you a rough version in a few weeks?" (beta pipeline)
